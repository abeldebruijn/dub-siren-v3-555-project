# Breadboard language V0.2

V0.2 adds singleton chips, discrete components, controls, routed `via` anchors,
and numbered annotations to the breadboard language. It is a source-language
specification for the Breadboard compiler; it does not add circuit simulation.

## Principles

- A program describes one breadboard (or uses the existing default board).
- All named things share one case-insensitive namespace.
- Components, wires, and annotations resolve to physical breadboard groups.
- Geometry is deterministic. Wires are orthogonal; component bodies may be
  diagonal.
- Errors prevent a Canonical Breadboard Model and SVG. Warnings do not.
- Existing source need not remain valid: V0.2 replaces reusable chip definitions
  and `place` with singleton chip declarations.

## Document order

Source is line-oriented, case-insensitive for keywords and names, and keeps the
existing comment, selector, colour, rail, board, and wire syntax.

Declarations occur in this order:

1. optional `breadboard`;
2. placed components: `chip`, `led`, `capacitor`, `resistor`, `button`,
   `potentiometer`, and `switch`;
3. `wire` declarations;
4. `annotation` declarations.

A declaration must precede every reference to it. Component properties may be
written in any order but cannot repeat; the formatter uses the order shown in
this document.

## Singleton chips

A chip declaration both defines and places exactly one chip. Its declared name
is the component instance name; a separate `place` declaration for that name is
an error.

```text
chip NE555 {
  height 4
  width 2
  color black
  pin 1 GND
  pin 3 OUT
  pin 8 VCC
} at R5 flip
```

- `at R<n>` is required and gives the footprint's top row.
- `height` is optional. When absent, it is half the highest declared pin number,
  which must therefore be even. An explicit height wins and may be odd. Sparse
  declarations are allowed.
- `width` is optional and defaults to `2` terminal columns.
- `color` is optional and defaults to black.
- `flip` retains its established chip-footprint and pin-numbering behaviour.
- A physical pin is referenceable as `NE555.3`; every declared alias is also
  referenceable, for example `NE555.OUT`. Reused aliases select all matching
  pins, as in the existing language.
- A pin reference resolves to a free hole in its terminal electrical group, not
  the physical hole occupied by the pin.
- Footprint overlap, covered-hole use, row overflow, and duplicate component
  names are errors.

## Wires and `via`

The established wire form remains:

```text
wire <source> --> <destination>
  [via <exact-point> [, <exact-point> ...]]
  [color <colour>]
  [saturation <percentage>]
```

Each `via` is an ordered, exact rail or terminal coordinate. It is a visual
anchor, never an electrical connection, and cannot be a pin reference or a
partial selector. The visible route must pass through every anchor. For every
diagonal leg between source, anchors, and destination, the compiler adds the
fewest 90-degree bends, applying the existing deterministic obstacle and
tie-break rules. A wire's endpoints alone are electrical connections.

## Two-terminal components

LEDs, capacitors, and resistors use `from` and `to` endpoints. Endpoints accept
the same resolved endpoint forms as wires, including component-pin references.

```text
led D1 from LP1 to LT-R5C1 {
  color red
  on true
  display-legs true
}

capacitor C1 from LT-R5C1 to LT-R8C1 {
  type electrolytic
  color blue
  capacitance 100
  max-voltage 16
  displayed capacitance max-voltage
}

resistor R1 from LT-R6C1 to RT-R6C1 {
  value 10k
  bands 4
}
```

- The component body runs directly between its resolved endpoints. It may be
  horizontal, vertical, or diagonal; it never acquires wire-style bends.
- Endpoint occupancy is strict. A body crossing a wire or another body is
  visual-only and allowed.
- The endpoints cannot resolve to the same electrical group.
- They also cannot both resolve to positive rails (`LP` or `RP`) or both to
  ground rails (`LG` or `RG`). Ordinary wires may bridge rails.
- The first endpoint is the positive/anode endpoint for polarised components.

### LED

- `color` defaults to red.
- `on` defaults to `false` and changes only the rendered state; it uses reduced
  saturation when off, never opacity.
- `display-legs` defaults to `true`.
- The SVG marks `+` and `-` beside the LED.
- An anode directly on `LG`/`RG`, or a cathode directly on `LP`/`RP`, is a
  warning. No circuit tracing or electrical simulation occurs.

### Capacitor

- `type` defaults to `ceramic`; accepted values are `ceramic`, `electrolytic`,
  `film`, `tantalum`, and `supercapacitor`.
- `color` is optional and otherwise determined by type.
- `capacitance` is a positive decimal value in microfarads; `max-voltage` is a
  positive decimal value in volts. Units and scientific notation are not used.
- `displayed` is optional and takes one or more of `capacitance` and
  `max-voltage`. A requested displayed value must be declared. The SVG prints
  `µF` and `V` with the corresponding values.
- Ceramic is a disc, electrolytic a cylinder, film a box, tantalum a bead, and
  supercapacitor a tall dual-ended cylinder. Electrolytic, tantalum, and
  supercapacitor variants show `+` and `-` and receive the LED polarity warning.

### Resistor

- `value` is required and is measured in ohms.
- A value is a positive integer or decimal with an optional no-space suffix:
  `k`/`K` multiplies by 1,000 and `m`/`M` multiplies by 1,000,000. Thus `470`,
  `4.7K`, and `1m` are valid; milli-ohms and other suffixes are not.
- `bands` defaults to `4` and accepts `4`, `5`, or `6`.
- Four bands encode two significant figures, multiplier, and gold tolerance;
  five bands encode three significant figures, multiplier, and gold tolerance;
  six add a brown temperature-coefficient band.
- The body is neutral tan and carries the derived bands, with no text value.
  A value that cannot be represented by the selected band count is an error.

## Placed controls

Controls occupy only their leg holes; their illustrated bodies do not reserve
additional board holes. All automatically placed terminal names and numbers
resolve to free holes in their terminal groups, like chip-pin references.

### Button

```text
button S1 {
  pins-per-side 2
  on false
} at R5
```

- `pins-per-side` is optional and defaults to `2`; it also gives the occupied
  row count.
- A button straddles the centre gap, at `LT-C1` and `RT-C1`.
- `left1…leftN` run top-to-bottom. Numeric pins run down the left, then up the
  right, so with the default: `1 = left1`, `2 = left2`, `3 = right2`, and
  `4 = right1`.
- Pins on one side are internally connected. `on true` connects the two sides;
  `on false` does not.

### Potentiometer

```text
potentiometer P1 {
  resistance 10k
  value 0.25
} at R5 right outside
```

- `resistance` defaults to `10k`; it uses the resistor ohm-value form.
- `value` defaults to `0.5` and is a decimal from `0` through `1`.
- Numeric pins `1` to `3` map to `low`, `wiper`, `high`, in top-to-bottom row
  order. `value 0` points toward low and `value 1` toward high.
- It uses `LT-C1` by default; `right` selects `RT-C1`.

### Switch

```text
switch S2 {
  options 3
  value 2
} at R10 outside
```

- `options` defaults to `2` and accepts integers from `2` upward.
- `value` defaults to `1` and must be an integer from `1` through `options`.
- Pin `1` is `common`; pins `2…n+1` are `option1…optionN`, in consecutive rows.
- It uses `LT-C1` by default; `right` selects `RT-C1`. Its canonical renderer
  presents the options as a line.

Buttons, potentiometers, and switches have neutral canonical bodies. State is
shown through mechanical position, not user-selected colour. They have no
rotation or `flip` modifier in V0.2. `outside` is valid for each control: legs
remain in the board, the body moves beyond the nearest board perimeter, and the
SVG viewbox expands to include it. Without `outside`, the body is drawn within
the board when possible.

Button and switch internal contacts participate in structural connectivity and
duplicate-connection diagnostics. Potentiometer value is visual-only.

## Annotations

```text
annotation 1 at NE555.OUT
```

An annotation is a unique positive integer anchored to an exact resolved rail,
terminal, chip-pin, or placed-component-pin endpoint. It has no electrical
meaning and occupies no hole. The SVG draws it as a small numbered circle beside
the target. Several annotations may share one target; deterministic offsets keep
them legible. Text annotations are out of scope.

## Rendering and diagnostics

The renderer draws, in order: board, wires, component legs and bodies, then
annotations and labels. Components and numbered circles therefore remain legible
above wires. An outside control expands the viewport; virtual wires remain
visible beyond the board perimeter.

Errors include invalid values, duplicate names/properties, invalid or unavailable
endpoints, placement overlap, forbidden two-terminal placements, invalid state,
and a `place` declaration for a singleton chip. Warnings include direct LED or
polarised-capacitor rail polarity mistakes and existing duplicate-connection or
wire-overlap conditions. Errors suppress canonical SVG; warnings do not.

No V0.2 feature simulates voltage, current, brightness, resistance networks, or
other circuit behaviour.
