Hold Trigger and turn the potentiometer. The LED repeats slowly or quickly, like the modulation-rate indicator in your dub siren.

## 1. Keep the complete circuit

Keep the LED on GP15 with its 1 kΩ resistor, button on GP14, and potentiometer wiper on GP26. No wires move.

The pot no longer sets brightness directly. It now sets the **rate** of a repeating event: your first low-frequency oscillator.

## 2. Turn 0…1 into milliseconds

The loop needs a time interval. This line maps the pot to `500…50` milliseconds:

```python
interval = int(500 - pot.value * 450)
```

- At `pot.value = 0.0`: interval is `500 ms`.
- At `pot.value = 0.5`: interval is `275 ms`.
- At `pot.value = 1.0`: interval is `50 ms`.

Before reading on, predict which end of the knob will flash fastest, and why a smaller interval makes the LED flash faster.

## 4. Play and test

1. Release button: LED remains off at every knob position.
2. Hold button with knob fully slow: count five flashes.
3. Keep holding and turn toward fast: rate changes immediately.
4. Release during a slow flash: LED turns off without waiting for the interval.

:::details The direction feels backwards
Change the mapping to `interval = int(50 + pot.value * 450)`. This changes software direction without moving the pot wires.
:::

:::details The LED appears dim at the fastest end
At high speed, separate flashes merge visually. That is normal. Slow it down and confirm distinct on/off changes return.
:::

## 5. Translate it to the synthesizer

The LED shows the LFO’s timing. Later the same repeating value will change pitch or another sound parameter. The pot becomes **Mod Rate**; the button remains **Trigger**.
