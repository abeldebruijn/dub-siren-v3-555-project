A physical knob becomes a software value—the basis of the dub siren’s **Pitch** control.

## 1. Know the three pins

Your B10K is a 10 kΩ linear potentiometer. Equal turns produce roughly equal changes in its measured value.

| Pin | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Outside pin | 3.3 V end |
| Centre pin | Wiper: moving output |
| Outside pin | Ground end |

## 2. Add the potentiometer

**Unplug USB before changing the circuit.** Keep the LED and button wiring from Lesson 2.

1. Connect one outside potentiometer pin to Pico `3V3(OUT)`.
2. Connect the centre wiper to `GP26 / ADC0`.
3. Connect the other outside pin to `GND`.
4. Trace it aloud: “3V3, wiper to GP26, ground.”

<LessonThreeCircuit />

The new potentiometer wires are vivid. The existing LED and button wires are muted.

:::danger Protect the Pico
Use `3V3(OUT)`, never `VBUS` or `VSYS`. An ADC input must not receive more than 3.3 V.
:::

:::details Why did a modulo version wrap around?
`% 5.0` means “remainder after division by five.” It does not remove decimals. At the top, `5.0 % 5.0` becomes `0.0`.
:::

## 4. Find all five positions

Turn slowly from one end to the other. Confirm that the Shell shows `0`, `1`, `2`, `3`, then `4`.

- **Direction feels backwards:** unplug USB and exchange only the two outside wires.
- **Value never changes:** check the centre wiper wire.
- **Numbers flicker at a boundary:** small analogue variations are normal. A later lesson can stabilize them.

Only GP26, GP27, and GP28 are exposed analogue inputs on the Pico. We start with GP26, also called ADC0.

## 5. Map input to output

Keep every wire in place. Now combine all three controls:

- `GP14 → button → GND` becomes **Trigger**.
- `3V3 → potentiometer → GND` creates a safe variable voltage.
- `wiper → GP26` becomes **Mod Amount**.
- `GP15 → 1 kΩ → LED → GND` displays the result.

`led.value` accepts the same `0.0…1.0` range produced by `pot.value`. The Pico rapidly switches the LED to create apparent brightness levels. This is pulse-width modulation, or PWM.

## 6. Play the control

1. Release the button and turn the knob: the LED stays off.
2. Hold the button and turn slowly: brightness follows the knob.
3. Release: the LED turns off immediately.
4. Hold again: the current knob position determines brightness.

The button acts like the siren’s **Trigger**. The potentiometer value can later become **Mod Amount**.

## 7. Safely change the LED colour

**Unplug USB.** Remove only the red LED. Insert a yellow or green LED in the same holes and orientation:

- Long leg toward the resistor and GP15.
- Short leg toward GND.
- Keep the 1 kΩ resistor in series.

Reconnect USB and repeat the test. Different colours may appear brighter at the same value. Do not use the starter-pack white LED here; its forward voltage is too close to 3.3 V for this beginner circuit.

:::details The replacement LED does not light
Unplug USB and rotate the LED 180°. LEDs conduct in one direction. Then verify that the resistor remains in series rather than being bypassed.
:::
