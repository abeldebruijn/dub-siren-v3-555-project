Bit operations, slowly

Understand exactly how & 1 and >> 1 turn a channel number into the S0 and S1 signals for the 4051.

Today’s win Given any 4051 channel number, you can explain how the code finds the correct S0 and S1 values.

1. A bit is one on/off value

A normal decimal digit has ten possibilities: 0 through 9. A binary digit—called a bit—has only two:

  • 0 means low or off.
  • 1 means high or on.

Each binary position has a value. From right to left, the first three positions are worth 1, 2 and 4:

Channel 2 is 010 because 0×4 + 1×2 + 0×1 = 2. Conveniently, the three bits directly describe the 4051 selector pins: S2 S1 S0.

2. & 1 keeps the rightmost bit

& means bitwise AND. At each position, the result is 1 only when both input bits are 1.

Left bitRight bitAND result
000
010

The number 1 is binary 001. It is used as a mask: zeros hide the left positions and the final one keeps the rightmost position.

channel 2:  010
mask 1:   & 001
result:     000

S0 = 0

For channel 1, the rightmost bit is on:

channel 1:  001
mask 1:   & 001
result:     001

S0 = 1

3. >> 1 moves bits right

>> is the right-shift operator. The number after it says how many positions to move. Bits falling off the right edge disappear.

before shift:  010
after >> 1:   001
                  ↑
          old S1 is now on the right

After shifting, use the same & 1 mask to keep that rightmost bit:

channel 2:       010
shift right:     001
mask 1:        & 001
result:          001

S1 = 1
Read the code from inside out

For (channel >> 1) & 1:

  1. Shift channel right by one position.
  2. Mask the result with 1.
  3. Send the resulting 0 or 1 to S1.

Why Lesson 12 does not calculate S2 S2 is physically connected to GND, so it is always 0. Lesson 12 only needs CH0–CH2 and calculates S0 and S1 in code.

5. Retrieve it from memory

Question 1 Channel 3 is binary 011. Before opening the answer, calculate channel & 1.
Show answer

011 & 001 = 001, so S0 = 1.

Question 2 For channel 2, what does (channel >> 1) & 1 produce?
Show answer

010 >> 1 = 001, then 001 & 001 = 001, so S1 = 1.

Done means

  • You can explain why & 1 extracts the rightmost bit.
  • You can explain why shifting right exposes the next bit.
  • You can derive S1 and S0 for channels 0–3.
