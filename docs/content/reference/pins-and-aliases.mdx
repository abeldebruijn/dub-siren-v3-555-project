# Pins and aliases

Every declared pin has a physical number and at least one readable alias.

```text
pin 2 OUT | SIGNAL | PWM
```

Here `OUT`, `SIGNAL`, and `PWM` all refer to physical pin 2. The first alias is printed as the pin label in the SVG.

## Pin numbering

For an unflipped chip, pin 1 starts at the top-left and increases down the left. Numbering continues from the bottom-right back toward the top-right. `flip` moves pin 1 to the right and mirrors that arrangement.

Pins may be sparse: a four-pin footprint may declare only pins 1, 2, and 4. An omitted pin still exists physically, but has no declared alias.

## Pin references

Given an instance named `U1`, use either its physical number or any declared alias:

```text
wire U1.2 --> RP1
wire U1.OUT --> RP1
wire U1.SIGNAL --> RP1
```

Pin references resolve to a free hole in the same terminal electrical group as the physical chip pin. They do not reuse the hole occupied by the chip leg.

## Alias rules

- Names and aliases are case-insensitive.
- An alias cannot start with a number.
- The same alias cannot be repeated on one pin.
- A numeric selector such as `U1.2` always means the physical pin number.
